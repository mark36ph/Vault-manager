Did you know the world's first computer bug was an actual bug?

It happened on September 9th, 1947, when engineers were working on the Harvard Mark II computer.

Suddenly, the machine stopped working.

After searching through thousands of electrical components, they discovered the problem...

A moth had become trapped inside one of the computer's relays.

The engineers carefully removed it and taped the moth into their logbook with the note, "First actual case of bug being found."

Although the word "bug" had already been used by engineers to describe mechanical problems, this unusual discovery made the term famous in the world of computing.

Believe it or not, that original logbook—with the moth still attached—is preserved at the Smithsonian Institution today.

Sometimes, the biggest problems really do start with the smallest bugs.
