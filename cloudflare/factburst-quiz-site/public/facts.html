Science

Why is the sky blue?

Sunlight looks white to our eyes, but it contains a mixture of visible wavelengths. When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, molecules in the air scatter the shorter blue wavelengths more strongly than the longer red wavelengths.

Blue light therefore reaches our eyes from many directions across the daytime sky. At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through more atmosphere, allowing more of the shorter wavelengths to be scattered away and leaving the direct light looking warmer.

Space

Why does the Moon have phases?

The Moon does not produce its own visible light; it reflects sunlight. As the Moon travels around Earth, the portion of its sunlit half that is visible from Earth changes.

That changing view produces the familiar phases: new moon, crescent, quarter, gibbous and full moon. The complete cycle from one new moon to the next takes about 29.5 days.

History

What made the printing press important?

Movable-type printing in Europe is strongly associated with Johannes Gutenberg's fifteenth-century press. Printing made it possible to reproduce texts much more quickly and consistently than hand copying alone.

Printed books helped ideas travel between cities and communities, supported wider literacy, and contributed to major changes in education, religion, science and public debate. Printing did not create these changes by itself, but it greatly increased the speed and scale at which information could circulate.

Geography

Why are time zones needed?

Earth rotates from west to east, so different longitudes experience noon, sunrise and sunset at different times. A single global clock would make local times difficult to use in everyday life.

Modern time zones divide the world into regions that use related local clock times. The idealised system is based on 24 hours for one rotation, although real-world borders and political decisions mean time-zone boundaries do not simply follow lines of longitude.

Nature

Why are leaves usually green?

Plants use chlorophyll to capture light energy for photosynthesis. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue portions of visible light particularly well, while reflecting more green light back toward our eyes.

That reflected green light is why many healthy leaves appear green. Leaf colour can change when seasons alter the amounts and types of pigments present in the leaf.

Technology

What does the word “algorithm” mean?

An algorithm is a defined procedure for solving a problem or carrying out a task. It can be as simple as a set of instructions for sorting objects or as complex as a mathematical process used by a computer program.

Algorithms are not limited to computers. People use step-by-step procedures in everyday activities too. In computing, however, algorithms are especially useful because computers can execute precise instructions repeatedly and at very high speed.

Mathematics

Why does a circle use π?

For every circle, the ratio of its circumference to its diameter is the same constant. That constant is called pi, written as π, and is approximately 3.14159.

Because π connects a circle's circumference and diameter, it appears in many formulas involving circles. For example, the circumference is 2πr and the area is πr², where r is the radius.

Music

What is a musical scale?

A musical scale is an ordered sequence of pitches. Different scales use different patterns of intervals, giving music particular melodic and harmonic characteristics.

The major scale is one familiar example in Western music. Its pattern of whole and half steps creates the sequence used by countless melodies, while other traditions and musical systems use many different scales and tuning approaches.
